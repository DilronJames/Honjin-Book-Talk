Case Overview / Exposition

The murders of Kenzo and Katsuko Ichiyanagi present the structure of an impossible crime. The newlyweds are discovered dead inside a locked room, with no clear evidence of how the murderer could have escaped. There is a bloodstained sword in the courtyard outside the annex along with an absence of footprints. The evidence points toward a mysterious three-fingered man which, all in all, makes everything very hard to explain.

The reality of the crime demonstrates that evidence is not always as reliable as it may seem. Throughout the investigation, convincing clues lead investigators astray from the ultimate conclusion. The interesting elements of the mystery lie not exclusively with the killer, but with the manipulation that conceals the killer.

This case archive examines the crime scene, the significance of Kenzo’s diaries, the involvement of the suspects, and Kindaichi’s deductions to understand how such an elaborate ploy was carried out.

Crime Scene
Reconstruction

The crime scene of the Ichiyanagi murders presents several unusual circumstances that make the possibility of an outside murderer difficult to explain. Kenzo and Katsuko are discovered dead inside a locked-room annex. The bloodstained murder weapon is found outside, in the courtyard. The absence of footprints in the snow surrounding the sword complicates the investigation; there appears to be no possible way for the murderer to have escaped. On top of this, around the time the murders were said to take place, sounds from a koto were heard being played.

Secrets behind the killerReveal
Kenzo murdered Katsuko before taking his own life. He intended arrangements to make it look like a double murder committed by an outsider.
The sword mechanismReveal
A mechanism involving a koto string and a waterwheel pulled the murder weapon outside the annex. It was meant to create the illusion that someone carried it away.
The missing footprintsReveal
Unexpected snowfall covered the misleading footprints that Kenzo had intended to leave as part of his deception.

Crime Scene Significance

The significance of the crime scene lies in how the physical evidence led investigators to make incorrect assumptions. Instead of questioning a potential outside murderer’s existence, they tried to explain how a murderer could have escaped the locked-room mystery.

Evidence Locker

The investigation of the Ichiyanagi murders reveals that evidence is not always able to be trusted. Several clues seemed to identify an outside murderer, but closer examination revealed contradictions that challenged the investigators’ original judgements.

Kindaichi's Final
Deduction

Kindaichi solves the mystery by questioning the evidence and what other people say about it. Rather than accepting a seemingly impossible crime, he tries to piece together a possible scenario. He considers whether the crime scene as he sees it was constructed to lead him toward an incorrect conclusion.

01

The weapon’s location did not prove that a murderer escaped.

02

The fingerprints didn’t prove the three-fingered man was responsible.

03

The altered diaries might have created a manipulated false narrative.

04

The second attack reinforced the apparent impossible crime and seemed ‘too perfect’.

Final Detective ConclusionReveal
Kindaichi determines that Kenzo killed Katsuko before taking his own life; Saburo manipulated the evidence to conceal the truth for money and his own ego. The impossible escape was an illusion created through the sword mechanism and structured clues.

Literary Analysis

Pride & Social Expectations

Kenzo’s excessive obsession with meticulous perfection and, by extension, purity shows the consequences of rigid social expectations and a society that emphasizes class distinctions. His refusal to accept Katsuko or call off the marriage shows how concerned he is with his public perception and honor, even more than another individual’s life. Kenzo is unable to admit he was wrong to his family or dissolve the marriage.

Evidence & Interpretation

Through the book, Yokomizo demonstrates that evidence can be genuine but still imply or support an incorrect conclusion. The manipulation of evidence, such as Kenzo’s diaries and the three-fingered man’s fingerprints, reveals how the interpretation of evidence can be just as important as the evidence itself.

Intelligence Without Morality

Saburo’s fascination with detective fiction influences his own involvement in the crime. His central place in the crime puts him in a prime situation to demonstrate his skills and intelligence. His willingness to exploit a tragedy for his own personal benefit reveals his lack of moral responsibility. He acts almost as a mystery writer controlling the inside of the story.

Yokomizo's Misdirection

Yokomizo uses the locked-room setting and misleading evidence to encourage readers to make the same assumptions as the investigators and hence believe in an impossible crime. By leading readers toward an incorrect conclusion with the evidence, he challenges them to look at the story from different angles to understand what really happened.

Final Interpretation

The significance of The Honjin Murders extends beyond its unexpected solution. The author demonstrates the influence of pride on human behavior, and he shows that evidence can be taken in many different ways. Ultimately, the book encourages readers to question appearances and not accept the conclusions that they initially suggest.
